AI-Powered Printed Electronics

How can a material read the way we move?
A sheet of mulberry paper can do more than carry words. Contact and separation generate electrical signals; machine learning then looks for patterns. Our featured study explores how paper properties affect this signal and its use in classifying foot health conditions.

Current Research Focus

Our research connects materials science and printed electronics with the wider vision of human–robot interaction. We study flexible sensing, biosensors, information storage and energy devices, alongside AI-assisted analysis and printing processes. These individual research directions inform the interfaces that may connect people and machines.

Computational Simulation

Experiments show what happens.
Simulation shows why.

We run DFT and COMSOL in-house, from the electronic structure of atoms to stress and electric potential across whole devices. In 13 of our published papers these calculations explain why our materials and devices behave as they do. Molecular dynamics (MD) is coming soon.

DFT

Å · Atoms and electrons
Density functional theory

Why does this material behave this way?

  • Charge transfer at interfaces (charge density difference, Bader charge)
  • Dipole moment and polarization
  • Adsorption and binding energy of molecules such as O₂ and glucose
  • Electronic structure (DOS/PDOS) and how adsorption shifts it
  • Band structure, work function, doping, defect and surface models

Molecular dynamics

How do molecules and ions move and pack?

  • Diffusion coefficients and ion transport in electrolytes
  • Polymer–filler interface interactions
  • Mechanical and thermal behavior of polymer chains

COMSOL

µm–mm · Devices and fields
Finite element multiphysics

How does the device respond to force, charge and ions?

  • Piezoelectric potential under load, compared across structures
  • Crack growth and strain in printed sensors under tension
  • Triboelectric potential and electric field distribution
  • Stress in bending, pressing and microneedle insertion
  • Diffusion, reaction and heat transfer in sensors and batteries
